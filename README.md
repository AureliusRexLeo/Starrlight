# Starrlight

Home lab and network. Lab machines are named after historic battleships that match their roles (Except Kraken). Personal machines have personal names.


## Purpose

Starrlight grew out of frustration with subscriptions and cloud file services. Endless monthly costs and the risk of losing access to paid content made local control necessary.

The final push came when digital purchases proved temporary—content locked behind stores that could later remove it, and the broader shift away from physical media that left ownership uncertain.

An old physical media library already existed, scattered across drives and backups. A NAS solved two problems at once: drop the subscriptions and serve media that was still owned. From there the project expanded.

Sharing with family and friends became practical. Hosting files and photos followed. Game servers for a few friends, better network security, and reliable local performance came next.

What started as one box quickly outgrew a single machine. Separate roles (storage, media, networking, AI, security) made more sense. Sixteen years of building and tinkering provided the foundation.

Networking had always been interesting but never urgent until the lab needed it. An injury created the time to research and plan properly—roughly three months of focused work to reach the current design. 

The same effort doubles as hands-on practice toward IT certifications and a possible career shift, something suggested since high school.

The lab is documented here so the plan, progress, and decisions stay clear. Learning and career use developed alongside the build rather than as the original goal.
