# Notes & Background


This page is mostly for my own reference and for anyone curious about the non-critical details. It keeps the “what and why” from getting lost.


## How Starrlight started

I got tired of subscriptions. The costs kept stacking and I was already using cloud storage just to move files between devices. Then digital “purchases” started disappearing. 

Content I had paid for vanished when stores shut down or removed titles. The bigger shift away from physical media made it obvious that a lot of what people buy digitally was never really owned. 

That was the last straw—especially once discs themselves started getting phased out as a reliable option. I already had a decade-old media library sitting on random hard drives and backups. 

A NAS looked like the clean way to fix both problems: stop paying for access and actually use the media I still had. Once that idea was in place it grew. I could share with family and friends. 

I could host files and photos instead of relying on outside services. Then game servers for a few friends who still play older or obscure titles.

Then making sure the home network stayed secure and fast. Local AI was already something I tinkered with; separating it from the main PC so it could grow made sense.

Everything was originally going to live on one box. The hardware requirements kept climbing, so the roles split. Storage, media playback, networking, AI, and security each got their own direction.

I’ve been building and tinkering since my teens—about sixteen years of putting machines together. Networking was always something I wanted to learn properly but never had a concrete reason until the lab needed it. An injury gave me the time to sit down and research the whole thing. Three months of reading, comparing, and planning got it to the point documented here.

While the lab comes together I’m also looking at certifications if the budget allows. People have told me since high school that IT work would fit.

Building and documenting this gives a practical way to study and show the work.


The device pages, parts list, and budget track the actual hardware and decisions. This page holds the longer context so it doesn’t disappear scatter across a million notes.
